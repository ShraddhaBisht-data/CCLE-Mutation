🧬 About Mutation

A mutation is a change in the DNA sequence. Mutations can occur naturally or due to different biological and environmental factors.

In cancer, some mutations can affect genes involved in cell growth, DNA repair, and cell survival, potentially contributing to uncontrolled cell growth.

The CCLE (Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia) provides molecular data from cancer cell lines, making it useful for studying mutation patterns and cancer biology.

---

🔬 Project

This project explores the CCLE mutation dataset to understand:

- 🧬 Which genes are frequently mutated
- 📊 How mutations are distributed across samples
- 🔎 Common mutation/variant patterns
- 🧹 Data quality and missing information
- 📈 Relationships and patterns visible through EDA

---

💡 Key Insights

The analysis focuses on identifying:

Gene-level patterns
Some genes appear much more frequently in mutation records than others.

Mutation diversity
The dataset contains different types and classifications of genetic alterations.

Sample variation
Mutation patterns can vary considerably between different cancer cell lines.

Data quality
Missing values and incomplete annotations are important considerations when working with genomic datasets.

Research potential
Frequently occurring mutation patterns can help identify areas for further biological investigation, although frequency alone does not establish clinical significance.
